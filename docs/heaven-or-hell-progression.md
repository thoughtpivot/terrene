# Heaven Or Hell: Character Creation and Progression

## Overview

This doc covers character creation and every progression system in Heaven Or Hell. It keeps the structure of Deepwoken's systems, as documented on the Deepwoken wiki, and changes only the flavour, to angels and Heaven. Where it departs from how Deepwoken works, the text says so.

It started as a proposal for Dan. Dan has since decided the points listed under [Decided by Dan](#decided-by-dan), and he kept the lore hook (the Tenth), the eight Choirs, the angelic names, the biblically grounded content and the Tier 0–5 corruption tiers. Everything else is still a proposal. Every number in this doc is a starting value to tune in play, not a fact.

It's the companion to [heaven-or-hell-plan.md](./heaven-or-hell-plan.md), which holds the decisions, the world and run structure, the technical approach and the milestones. Where the two docs touch, this one has the detail and the plan has a summary.

**How it fits the plan's death rules.** Lives work as Deepwoken's do, and Dan has kept them as they are: the first time you're struck down your halo dims (Deepwoken's stained health), and the second time casts you out and wipes the run. That's separate from personal **Stain** (section 11), which is corruption you take on for power. A dimmed halo is about lives. A cracked, reddening halo is about Stain. The two can happen together, and neither causes the other.

## Decided by Dan

1. **Right-click is always the feint.** R is the only critical input. There are no right-click or middle-click criticals. Feints must work against the AI, not only in PvP, so enemies read your wind-ups and can be baited (section 14).
2. **Choirs are chosen freely** at character creation, not rolled. Rare Choir variants are unlocked through Testimony and Remembrance, not rolled (section 2).
3. **Solo only, for now.** Multiplayer is out of scope. Every system here works against NPCs: the Penitent hunts marked corrupted angels, the Host of the Gate's ranks are NPC-based, and Trumpet Progress has no PvP path.
4. **No strength cap per tier.** There's no Ascension cap tied to corruption. Heaven decays at its own rate, driven by time, and the challenge is to keep pace by growing stronger. So corruption rises at a steady, designed rate, and sealing a breach does not lower it. Sealing slows the local spread and protects a region and its rewards (section 10). *This reading of "decays at its own rate" is an interpretation that Dan can adjust.* Rising corruption still opens up more systems.
5. **Every tier brings** harder enemies, stronger weapons (better loot, higher weapon tiers and rarer weapons) and harder progression with bigger rewards (section 10).
6. **Full Stain ends the run.** At 100 Stain you fall out of Heaven as a fallen angel, and the run is wiped. Testimony and Remembrance still carry over (section 11).
7. **Lives stay as they are.** The first time you're struck down dims your halo, and the second wipes you.
8. **Starting weapons are basic:** sword, spear, knife and mace. The Flaming Sword and the other named weapons are found or earned as you progress, gated by corruption tier and quests (section 13).
9. **Boons and Burdens** is the name for Deepwoken's Boons and Flaws.
10. **Currencies are confirmed.** Visions are spent within a run and lost on a wipe. Testimony and Remembrance carry over. Grace is the mana for Hymns.

- The Vow of the Ophan's Eye's *Wheel within a Wheel* is now *Wheel in the Middle of a Wheel*. *Wheel within a Wheel* stays as a Trumpet.
- The Vow of the Flaming Sword's *Turning Every Way* is now *Ring of Flame*. *Turning Every Way* stays as the Flaming Sword's critical.

**Why Boons and Burdens.** "Graces" would collide with Grace, the mana. "Blessings and Burdens" sounds more angelic, but Blessing is already a kind of Anointing. "Mercies" would collide with the Mercy Seat, and "Favours" sounds too close to Fervour. So it stays Boons and Burdens.

## Research notes: Deepwoken facts this design is built on

- **Character creation** runs in this order: Aspect, Attunements (up to two, or None for 10 free points), weapon class, Boons and Flaws (at least one and at most two of each), Origin, Appearance, and then the Echo Shop and Modifiers.
- **Aspects** are rolled at random and cost money to reroll. Each gives +3 to one attribute and +2 to another, plus small innate Talent cards. Their in-game "variants" mostly change colour, and a few rare variants are only given out by the developers. Most downsides come from the Flaws you pick, not from the Aspect itself.
- **Attributes and Power:**
  - There are six core attributes, split into Body (Strength, Fortitude, Agility) and Mind (Intelligence, Willpower, Charisma).
  - There are three weapon attributes (Heavy, Medium, Light) and the Attunement attributes.
  - Each attribute has a soft cap of 75 and a hard cap of 100, which you reach through an "Unbound" quest.
  - Each Power level gives 15 points, up to Power 20, for 330 points in total (15 at creation, 5 from the Aspect, 10 from the Attunement).
  - Intelligence gives 2 Ether per point and Charisma gives 1.5. Willpower raises Sanity and Tempo.
- **Mantras:**
  - You get them from Whisper Draws on certain Power-ups. There is always a "Roll 2" card that gives two Talents instead of a Mantra.
  - Equip slots are 3 Combat, 1 Support, 1 Mobility and 1 Wildcard. Mantras have levels 1 to 5, unlocked at 20, 40, 60 and 80 points of investment.
  - Modifier stones trade one stat for another (size for speed, range for size, and so on). "Sparks" change how a Mantra behaves. Every modifier except Sparks raises the Ether cost.
  - Some support Mantras use "Ritual Casting", a short sequence of key presses.
- **Ether** regenerates during combat only while you have **Tempo**. With 40 or more Tempo, G performs a **Vent**.
- **Oaths:**
  - You can hold one at a time. They don't take up Talent slots.
  - They are unlocked by stat requirements or quests.
  - They are broken at Yun'Shul, often permanently.
  - Oath Mantras never level up.
  - A fully progressed Oath can be skipped on later characters for 10 Knowledge.
- **Talents:**
  - They come in hands of 4 to 5 cards, pick one. Rarities are Common, Rare and Advanced, with about two Rares per Power-up.
  - Some hands include a "Fold" card that saves the hand for later. **Aces** let you ban a card, guarantee one, or keep one.
  - Oath, Quest and Origin Talents are free. Some Talents are mutually exclusive.
- **Enchantments:**
  - Enchant Stones go on weapons and Grease goes on armour. Equipping an enchanted item soulbinds it, and you can't stack the same enchantment twice.
  - They come in two kinds: **Blessings** (no real downside) and **Curses** (an upside plus a matching downside).
- **Deep Shrines** cost **Knowledge**, a currency tied to one character that is lost on wipe:
  - **Order**: averages your stats. Costs 10, can be used once, needs Power 8 or higher and no Oath, and can't take more than 25 points from a core or weapon stat.
  - **Mastery**: costs 1 to refund an attribute point (three at most) or to move a Trait point.
  - **Blasphemy**: three instant Power-ups plus a Flaw.
  - **Division**, **Unification** and **Chance** also exist.
- **Resonance (the "Bell"):**
  - It is pressed with C.
  - You build up a hidden "Progress" stat from bosses, the Depths or PvP. If you die in the Depths with full Progress, you get "divine intervention": a full heal and a hand of three cards.
  - Rarities are Normal, Corrupted (stronger with one downside such as Wither or Mania), Legendary, and Drowned (corrupted Legendary).
  - Rerolling gives the "Tarnished" Flaw.
- **Echoes:**
  - They are earned on wipe from one-time "Triumphs".
  - They buy small capped upgrades (for example, mob damage +2% per step, up to 8%) and unlocks such as starter weapons.
  - Optional difficulty "Modifiers" increase how many Echoes you earn.
  - Learned Attunements and fighting styles stay unlocked for the whole account.
- **Origins** set where you spawn, your allegiance and your progression bonus. You start with one and unlock the others.
- **Murmurs:** you can have one, and the three are named after music terms (Ardour, Tacet, Rhythm).
- **Combat controls:**
  - Right-click (M2) during a wind-up is a feint. M2 also cancels Mantras and rolls.
  - The critical is R or the middle mouse button (M3), and most criticals can't be feinted.
  - Holding F blocks and builds posture. When posture overflows, the next blocked hit causes a guard break.
  - The M1 chain is usually three hits followed by a flourish.

Heaven Or Hell is solo, so the PvP parts of these systems (PvP Progress for the Bell, player bounties) are left out or turned into NPC versions.

## 1. Character creation flow

**The lore reason you're a lesser angel.** In the War in Heaven, the dragon's tail "drew the third part of the stars of heaven" (Revelation 12:4, 7–9). Heaven refilled the gaps in its choirs with lesser angels, kindled from the light of the choirs that stayed loyal. They are called **the Tenth**, after Isaiah 6:13: "yet in it shall be a tenth, and it shall return." You are one of them. You carry an *echo* of a higher choir, not its full rank, which is why no player is ever a true Seraph or Cherub. "It shall return" also explains the wipe: when Heaven resets, the Tenth is kindled again.

The steps follow Deepwoken's order:

1. **Choir (the Aspect).** Choose freely from the 8 Choirs (section 2). Each gives +3 and +2 to two attributes, a signature passive, two innate Gifts and a flaw. Unlike Deepwoken, nothing is rolled. Rare variants appear here once you've unlocked them with Testimony.
2. **Tongues (the Attunements).** Choose up to 2 of the 4 base Tongues, or none for **Wordless**, which gives 10 free points like Deepwoken's Attunement-less option.
3. **Arms (weapon class).** Choose a basic starting weapon: the sword, spear or knife, or the mace once Testimony unlocks it (section 13). Named weapons, such as the Flaming Sword, are never starting weapons.
4. **Boons and Burdens (Boons and Flaws).** Pick at least one and at most two of each.
   - Example Boons: *Tireless Wing* (more glide stamina), *Psalmist* (an extra Feather on every other Hymn Draw), *Unseen* (better stealth).
   - Example Burdens: *Clipped* (no air dash), *Doubting* (more Sanctity damage, like Manic), *Tarnished Halo* (enemies spot you more easily, like Obvious), *Unlettered* (slower experience and fewer Visions; counts as 2 points, like Simple).
5. **Origin ("where you were made").** This sets your spawn point, starting gear, allegiance and progression bonus. Every Origin works solo, against NPCs. Example Origins:
   - **The Outer Courts** (the default, like Castaway). Basic gear and the most common starting zone.
   - **The Lone Vigil** (like Lone Warrior). You spawn inside a trial. Clearing it grants *Mark of the Vigil*.
   - **Breach-Warden** (like Deepbound). You gain more experience inside Hell breaches and less outside them, and start with a recall item.
   - **Host of the Gate** (like Authority Ensign). You join the Cherubim's guard at the Gate of Eden. Ranks and divisions are earned through NPC commissions, and you fight beside allied angel NPCs. The cost is that leaving your post too long gets you branded a deserter.
   - **Scribe of Names** (like Ignition Delver). You progress through the archive's commissions and boards.
   - **The Penitent** (like Voidwalker). A bounty board at the Mercy Seat posts marked corrupted angels, which are NPC elites. Each marked angel you slay pays Visions and cleanses 5 Stain. There are no player bounties.
   - Every Origin except the Outer Courts is unlocked through Testimony or Witnesses.
6. **Appearance**, following Deepwoken's Body, Face and Aspect Features sections:
   - **Wings:** count from 2 to 6, shown as folded or clipped, with feather, ember or glass texture.
   - **Halo:** ring, broken ring, thorned, wheel or crown shape, in white, gold or silver light.
   - **Eyes:** pupil-less glow or veiled. Optional extra eyes on the wings for the Wheelborn.
   - **Robes:** linen (after "the man clothed with linen" in Ezekiel 10:2), sash, cowl.
   - **Marks:** small sigils specific to each Choir.
   - **Style note:** the body stays near-monochrome; only the halo, eyes and wing edges glow white and gold.
7. **Testimony Shop and Penances.** Spend meta currency and turn on difficulty Penances (section 12). Then the angel "kindles" into the world.

**Starting attributes:** 15 free points plus the Choir's +5 (or +10 more free points if you chose Wordless). This matches Deepwoken.

## 2. Choirs (the Aspects)

Choirs are chosen freely. Each one follows the same template: lore, look, stats, a signature passive, two innate Gifts, a flaw, synergies, a recommended starter weapon and a rare variant. The stat numbers mirror Deepwoken (+3 and +2). The flaws are a small departure from Deepwoken, whose Aspects rarely have built-in downsides, so they stay light, and Boons and Burdens remain the main source of trade-offs.

Every body is dark and near-monochrome. Only the halo, the eyes, the wing edges and each Choir's marks glow, in white or gold, so the player always reads against the near-black world (see the plan's art direction).

### How the Choirs are balanced

Each Choir spends the same budget, in rough points that are a starting value to check in play:

| Part | Budget |
| --- | --- |
| Stats | +5 in total, as +3 and +2. No two Choirs share the same pair |
| Signature passive | About 3 points: worth about one Rare Gift |
| Two innate Gifts | About 1 point each: worth about one Common Gift each |
| Flaw | About −1 point: costs about one Common Gift, and can always be played around |

Further rules:
- **No Choir is best at two of the three jobs** (offence, defence and utility). Choirs whose passive is mostly out of combat (Lordling, Wheelborn, Banneret) get a combat part to their passive or a stronger combat Gift to make up for it.
- **Swiftness never gets the +3.** Roll distance and i-frames are the cheapest defence in a parry-and-roll combat system, so Swiftness only comes as a +2.
- **Every passive touches combat** through at least one of parry, posture or Hymns, so no pick is wasted in a fight.
- **The check** is to fight the M2 and M3 training demons with each Choir and the same starter weapon. Time to kill and damage taken should land within about 10% of each other. A Choir outside that band gets its numbers tuned, not its identity.

| Choir | Stats | Leans toward | Recommended starter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kindled | Faith +3, Voice +2 | Offence through parries, and fire | Sword |
| Four-Faced | Might +3, Steadfast +2 | Posture, and adapting between rests | Spear |
| Wheelborn | Wisdom +3, Swiftness +2 | Awareness, and learning enemies fast | Knife |
| Lordling | Wisdom +3, Voice +2 | Hymn casting, and navigation | Spear |
| Virtuant | Faith +3, Swiftness +2 | Statuses, and rolling through attacks | Knife |
| Bindborn | Might +3, Faith +2 | Breaking demons' posture | Sword (mace once unlocked) |
| Banneret | Voice +3, Steadfast +2 | Fighting beside allied angels | Spear |
| Watcher-kin | Steadfast +3, Wisdom +2 | Surviving, at the cost of Stain | Sword |

Across the eight Choirs, Wisdom and Faith each total 8 points, Steadfast and Voice 7, Might 6 and Swiftness 4.

### Kindled (an echo of the Seraphim, Isaiah 6)

**Lore.** The Kindled were lit from the altar's overflow, from the same fire that gave the seraph "a live coal in his hand, which he had taken with the tongs from off the altar" (Isaiah 6:6). They sing without stopping, and when they fight, the song turns to fire. Other Tenth call them reckless, because the light that makes them strong also makes them easy to see.

**Look.** Four small wings, where the true seraph has six (Isaiah 6:2). A black robe flecked with embers, coal-white eyes, and a gold halo with a single ember set in it, which glows while the Live Coal is held.

**Stats.** Faith +3, Voice +2.

**Signature passive: Live Coal.**
- *Holding it:* you carry one Live Coal, shown as the ember in your halo.
- *Trigger:* a successful parry while you hold the Coal. A block doesn't count, and a parry that whiffs or is feinted doesn't spend it.
- *Effect:* the parry deals +20% posture damage, purges your longest-lasting ailment, and applies 2 stacks of Sear to the attacker.
- *Recharge:* 25 seconds after it's spent, or at once when you pray at an altar.
- *With Hymns:* a Seraph-Fire Hymn that hits a target carrying Sear from the Live Coal refunds 10% of its Grace.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Unconsumed:* Sear and fire damage against you is 15% lower.
- *Altar-Kept:* praying at an altar also restores 25% of your Grace.

**Flaw: Burning Bright.**
- Hell enemies notice you from 20% farther away while the Coal is lit, and 10% farther while it's spent.
- *Playing around it:* spend the Coal on a parry before you sneak, approach through unlit ground, or take the *Unseen* Boon to offset it.

**Synergies.** Seraph-Fire for Sear stacking, and Radiance once unlocked. The sword's even parry timing makes the Coal easy to land, and the Flaming Sword and Censer Flail later fit the fire theme.

**Recommended starter:** the sword.

**Rare variant: Ashen Kindled.** Soot-black wings and white embers. Live Coal also Hushes the attacker for 1 second, but loses the +20% posture damage. Unlocked for 40 Testimony once three demon types are Mastered in the bestiary.

### Four-Faced (an echo of the Cherubim, Ezekiel 1 and 10)

**Lore.** The Four-Faced were kindled at the Gate, among the guardians of thresholds. Each carries the four faces of the living creatures, "the face of a man, and the face of a lion… the face of an ox… the face of an eagle" (Ezekiel 1:10), and wears whichever one the day needs. They're steady and stubborn, and they don't like to turn aside.

**Look.** A mask of four faces that turns between them, and "burnished brass" hoof-greaves after "the sole of a calf's foot" (Ezekiel 1:7). The body is dark bronze-black, and the face being worn glows gold.

**Stats.** Might +3, Steadfast +2.

**Signature passive: Turning Face.** Each time you rest at an altar, you set one face, and it lasts until your next rest:
- *Lion:* your flourish deals +15% posture damage.
- *Ox:* posture damage you take while blocking is 10% lower.
- *Eagle:* you get +1 air dash charge.
- *Man:* +10 Voice for dialogue checks only, and allied angels' quest rewards are 10% larger.
- *With parry, posture and Hymns:* the Lion and Ox faces work on posture and the Eagle on movement. No face affects Hymns, so the Four-Faced lean on weapons.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Calf's Foot:* knockback you take moves you 25% less.
- *Living Creature:* the stun after you're guard-broken is 15% shorter (about 0.85 seconds instead of 1).

**Flaw: Unturning** ("they turned not when they went," Ezekiel 1:9).
- During an attack's wind-up, you can turn 30% less toward the mouse.
- You climb 20% more slowly.
- *Playing around it:* aim before you click, take a reach weapon such as the spear, and pick fights at chokepoints rather than in the open.

**Synergies.** Judgement-Thunder's short, committed strikes, and Firmament's walls to cover your flanks. The spear and mace work with Lion posture damage. Later, the Spear of the Host and the Censer Flail.

**Recommended starter:** the spear, whose reach makes up for Unturning.

**Rare variant: Brass-Shod Four-Faced.** You can't be knocked down, but you lose the Eagle face. Unlocked for 40 Testimony once you've reached Studied on Behemoth.

### Wheelborn (an echo of the Ophanim or Thrones, Ezekiel 1:15–21)

**Lore.** The Wheelborn are small wheel-spirits that once turned beside the throne. "The spirit of the living creature was in the wheels" (Ezekiel 1:20), and some of that spirit is in them. They see everything and forget nothing, which makes them the best learners among the Tenth and the most easily shaken.

**Look.** A halo that is a beryl-glowing ring set with eyes (Ezekiel 1:16–18), and a second slow ring that orbits the body. The body is dark grey, and the eyes glow white.

**Stats.** Wisdom +3, Swiftness +2.

**Signature passive: Rings of Eyes** ("their rings were full of eyes round about them four," Ezekiel 1:18).
- *Always on*, with no cooldown.
- Stealthed enemies and ambushes within 10 metres are outlined.
- The telegraph of an attack from outside your view (behind you or off-screen) shows 0.1 seconds earlier, so it's easier to parry.
- Bestiary progress toward Studied and Mastered builds 25% faster, including learning enemies' read traits (section 14).
- *With posture:* none. *With Hymns:* Hymns with the Seal of the Wheel orbit 15% faster.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Spirit in the Wheels:* the recovery after a roll is 10% shorter.
- *Whirling:* Grace regenerates 5% faster.

**Flaw: Seeing Too Much.**
- You lose 25% more Sanctity from horror sights and Hell auras.
- *Playing around it:* raise Faith, train with the Psalter, and don't linger inside Hell auras.

**Synergies.** Whirlwind and Judgement-Thunder for mobile fights. The knife's short parry window benefits most from the earlier telegraphs. Later, the Ophan Cestus.

**Recommended starter:** the knife.

**Rare variant: Sapphire Wheelborn.** Blue-white rings, after the sapphire throne in Ezekiel 1:26. Rings of Eyes also shows hostile casters' cooldowns, but loses the faster bestiary progress. Unlocked for 40 Testimony once 20 enemy types are Studied.

### Lordling (an echo of the Dominions)

**Lore.** In Christian angelology, the Dominions are traditionally said to "govern the movement of stars." The Lordlings carry a little of that order. They plot every road and count every hour, and when they cast, they cast first and cheaply. The other Tenth find them proud.

**Look.** A star-map pattern on a black mantle and a crown-shaped halo. The stars on the mantle glow faint gold.

**Stats.** Wisdom +3, Voice +2.

**Signature passive: Star-Reckoner.**
- *Navigation, always on:* discovered landmarks show their distance on the compass, and the night sky marks the nearest Shrine of the Throne.
- *Dominion, in combat:* the first Hymn you cast in each fight costs 25% less Grace. It resets once you've been out of combat for 10 seconds.
- *With parry and posture:* none.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Ordered Courses:* Hymn cooldowns are 5% shorter.
- *Charted:* at the start of each run, the map memory from Remembrance also marks every Shrine of the Throne site you've found before.

**Flaw: Aloof.**
- Allied angels charge 20% more for their services, unless you pass a Voice check (Voice 30).
- *Playing around it:* put points into Voice, which the +2 helps with, or rely on altars, which never charge.

**Synergies.** Seraph-Fire and Firmament for casting builds. The spear keeps enemies at a distance while you cast. Later, the Herald's Horn and the Bow of Light.

**Recommended starter:** the spear.

**Rare variant: Pole-Star Lordling.** Dominion applies to the first two Hymns in each fight, at 15% off each, and the night sky marks the nearest breach instead of a shrine. Unlocked for 40 Testimony once map memory covers every region.

### Virtuant (an echo of the Virtues)

**Lore.** The Virtues are the choir traditionally said to govern the elements and storms. The Virtuants were kindled in the storm-front of the war, and they still move like weather: fast, loud and charged. They pick up a Tongue faster than any other Choir, and Hell's Tongues stick to them just as fast.

**Look.** Wings made of stormcloud, with gold lightning veins running through them. The body is slate-dark, and the veins pulse when you roll.

**Stats.** Faith +3, Swiftness +2.

**Signature passive: Elemental Ease and Charged Step.**
- *Elemental Ease:* +1 Tongue point at creation, and Cantors charge 20% less.
- *Charged Step:* rolling through an enemy attack, so that the i-frames avoid a hit, gives your next Hymn +15% status buildup. It has an 8-second cooldown.
- *With parry and posture:* none. It rewards rolls, not parries.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Stormcloud Wings:* Whirlwind and Judgement-Thunder status buildup is 10% higher.
- *Lightning Veins:* in combat, Grace regenerates 10% faster while you hold 20 or more Fervour.

**Flaw: Lightning Rod.**
- Statuses from enemy Tongues build up on you 15% faster.
- *Playing around it:* keep your distance from casters, use Firmament barriers, and avoid the *Doubting* Burden.

**Synergies.** Judgement-Thunder and Whirlwind. The knife's fast chain keeps Fervour up for Lightning Veins. Later, the Bow of Light.

**Recommended starter:** the knife.

**Rare variant: Hail-Wing Virtuant.** Frost-white wings. Charged Step buffs Firmament instead, and Awe from your Hymns turns into Struck Still 10% sooner. Unlocked for 40 Testimony once a Firmament Hymn reaches level 3 on any character.

### Bindborn (an echo of the Powers)

**Lore.** The Powers are traditionally said to "have power over evil forces" and to restrain them. The Bindborn were made to hold Hell's things down. They're jailers, and demons know it: in any fight, the demons go for the Bindborn first.

**Look.** Chains of light wrapped around the forearms, and a ring-shaped halo with a gap in it. The body is iron-dark, and the chains glow gold.

**Stats.** Might +3, Faith +2.

**Signature passive: Restraint.**
- You deal +10% posture damage to demons, but not to corrupted angels. The bonus also applies to your parries' posture damage.
- When you guard-break a demon, chains of light hold it, so the stun lasts 0.3 seconds longer. This can happen to each demon once every 20 seconds.
- *With Hymns:* none.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Chains of Light:* your critical (R) deals 10% more damage to a guard-broken target.
- *Jailer's Grip:* gripping (finishing off) a downed demon is 20% faster.

**Flaw: Marked Jailer.**
- In fights with allied angel NPCs nearby, demons pick you as their target first.
- *Playing around it:* bait their commitments with feints (section 14), fight where only a few can reach you, and block with the Ox-like steadiness of a heavy weapon.

**Synergies.** Firmament, whose Awe holds a target in place, and Sacrifice once unlocked. The sword and mace break posture well. Later, the Censer Flail.

**Recommended starter:** the sword, or the mace once Testimony unlocks it.

**Rare variant: Chainless Bindborn.** Restraint works on corrupted angels too, but its posture bonus drops to +6%. Unlocked for 40 Testimony after slaying 5 marked corrupted angels across all runs.

### Banneret (an echo of the Principalities)

**Lore.** The Principalities are traditionally guardians of peoples and groups. The Bannerets carry that care. They fight best with other angels around them and lose heart alone. In a solo game, their allies are Heaven's own: the allied angel NPCs who fight at the breaches.

**Look.** A banner-cloth hanging from the wings and a lantern-shaped halo. The body is dark linen-grey, and the lantern glows gold.

**Stats.** Voice +3, Steadfast +2.

**Signature passive: Rally.**
- Allied angel NPCs within 12 metres regenerate posture 25% faster and follow you 50% longer.
- *Raise the Banner:* your Outcry also restores 15% posture to you and to every ally in range. It uses the Outcry's own cost, with no extra cooldown.
- *With Hymns:* support Hymns cast on allies cost 10% less Grace.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Ensign:* one more allied angel can follow you at a time.
- *Lantern Halo:* your light radius is 20% larger, which matters in a near-black world.

**Flaw: Of the Host.**
- Your Sanctity regenerates 40% more slowly with no ally within 12 metres.
- *Playing around it:* take allies along from altars and quests, rest often, and put a few points into Faith.

**Synergies.** Whirlwind, whose Hushed status controls casters, and Radiance once unlocked. The spear lets you fight from behind an ally. Later, the Herald's Horn.

**Recommended starter:** the spear.

**Rare variant: Ensign Banneret.** Rally also covers you alone: with no ally in range, you regenerate posture 10% faster. But allies follow you only 25% longer. Unlocked for 40 Testimony after completing the Host of the Gate's first rank on any character.

### Watcher-kin (an echo of the Guardian Angels)

**Lore.** The Christian angelology page notes that "the watchers were of this class." The Watcher-kin are kindled from a lineage that has fallen before. They keep watch better than anyone, and they're hard to put down. But Hell remembers them, and Stain takes to them easily.

**Look.** Grey-white wings, a veil, and a dim halo that flickers. The body is ash-dark, and the halo's flicker is white.

**Stats.** Steadfast +3, Wisdom +2.

**Signature passive: Keeper.**
- Allied angels you revive come back with 25% more HP.
- While you're knocked down, enemies take 30% longer to grip (finish) you.
- *Vigil:* when you get up after a knockdown, you have 0.5 seconds of hyperarmor. It has a 30-second cooldown.
- *With parry and Hymns:* none.

**Innate Gifts.**
- *Watchful:* the first ambush attack that hits you in each fight deals 30% less damage.
- *Veil:* standing still for 1 second improves your stealth by 10%.

**Flaw: the Old Fall.**
- You gain 25% more Stain from every source, but your Fallen Gifts are 10% stronger. This is a deliberate hook into the risk-and-reward system (section 11).
- *Playing around it:* stay at 0 Stain and take the Sanctified Gift pool, or lean in and use the stronger Fallen Gifts, watching the 100 limit.

**Synergies.** Sacrifice once unlocked, and Firmament barriers. The sword is a safe all-rounder for a defensive Choir. Later, the Flaming Sword, which suits a lineage of watchers at a gate.

**Recommended starter:** the sword.

**Rare variant: Seventh Watcher.** You start with 10 Stain, and Fallen Gifts can appear from Corruption Tier 1. Unlocked for 40 Testimony after any run that ended in the Fall.

## 3. Attributes

- **Form**, the Body category. Your Form value is your highest Form attribute.
  - **Might** (Strength): penetration, carry load, heavy-weapon requirements.
  - **Steadfast** (Fortitude): HP, posture-related requirements, resisting Hell's poisons.
  - **Swiftness** (Agility): parkour, roll distance, stealth.
- **Spirit**, the Mind category. Your Spirit value is your highest Spirit attribute.
  - **Wisdom** (Intelligence): +2 **Grace** (the Ether equivalent) per point, potions and incense, lore checks.
  - **Faith** (Willpower): **Sanctity** (Sanity) and **Fervour** (Tempo). Hell exposure drains Sanctity.
  - **Voice** (Charisma): +1.5 Grace per point, dialogue, and ally angel morale. You train it by singing to an audience instead of playing an instrument.
- **Arms** (the weapon attributes):
  - **Heavy Arms**, "of Judgement": maces, greatswords, censers.
  - **Medium Arms**, "of the Host": swords, spears.
  - **Light Arms**, "of the Herald": knives, gauntlets, bows.
- **Tongues** (the Attunement attributes): one per school in section 4.
- **Kept from Deepwoken:** the soft cap of 75; the hard cap of 100 through **Unsealing** quests (Deepwoken's Unbound); 15 points per **Ascension** (Power) up to 20, for 330 points in total; and training gear such as Prayer Beads, renamed *Psalter* (Faith) and *Wing-Weights* (Swiftness).
- **No cap per tier.** Ascension isn't capped by corruption tier. Section 10 gives the pace a player should aim for at each tier, as targets for balancing, not limits.

## 4. Tongues (the Attunements) and Hymns (the Mantras)

**Grace (the Ether equivalent)** is the mana for Hymns: the visible "glory" that blooms around the angel (Isaiah 6:3, "the whole earth is full of his glory"). It comes from Wisdom, Voice and gear. In combat it regenerates only while you have Fervour (Tempo), and it regenerates faster out of combat, both as in Deepwoken. A Vent is called an **Outcry**: press G with 40 or more Fervour.

**Hymns (the Mantras)** match the Deepwoken originals in structure:
- Slots: 3 Combat, 1 Support, 1 Mobility and 1 Wildcard.
- Hymns come from **Hymn Draws** on certain Ascensions, and each draw always includes a *Two Lesser Gifts* card (Roll 2).
- Levels 1 to 5 at 20, 40, 60 and 80 points, upgraded at **Cantors** (trainers).
- Right-click, the feint input, during a cast cancels the Hymn without refunding its Grace. A cancelled cast can bait an enemy that committed to dodging it (section 14).
- Support Hymns use **Liturgy** casting, a short key sequence like Deepwoken's Ritual Casting.

**Altar Coals (the modifiers)** are applied at a **Lectern of Psalms**. Every Coal raises the Grace cost; Seals do not.
- *Ember:* larger size, slower speed.
- *Beryl Lens:* longer range, smaller size (Ezekiel 1:16).
- *Amber:* longer duration, shorter range (Ezekiel 1:4).
- *Brass Filing:* faster speed, shorter duration (Ezekiel 1:7).
- *Sapphire:* bigger area of effect, shorter duration (Ezekiel 1:26).
- **Seals** are the equivalent of Sparks and change a Hymn's behaviour: *Seal of Pursuit* (it chases the target), *Seal of Bursting* (it detonates), *Seal of the Wheel* (it orbits you).

**The six Tongues.** The first four can be chosen at creation. The last two are quest-unlocked and then stay unlocked for the account, mirroring Shadowcast and Bloodrend.

1. **Seraph-Fire** (like Flamecharm)
   - Status: **Sear** (burning).
   - Hymns: *Coal from the Altar* (a thrown coal, 1★), *Tongs of the Seraph* (a grab that brands the target, 2★), *Burning Throne* (a pillar of fire for area denial, 3★).
   - Path at 40: **Purging Path**, where Sear can heal allies instead of harming them (Isaiah 6:7).
2. **Firmament** (like Frostdraw): crystal "as the colour of the terrible crystal" (Ezekiel 1:22).
   - Status: **Awe**, a slow that turns into **Struck Still**, a freeze.
   - Hymns: *Crystal Shard* (1★), *Firmament Wall* (a barrier, 2★), *Sea of Glass* (a field that slows, 3★).
3. **Judgement-Thunder** (like Thundercall): "out of the fire went forth lightning" (Ezekiel 1:13).
   - Status: **Shock**.
   - Hymns: *Flash of the Living Creatures* (a lightning dash, after "ran and returned as… a flash of lightning," Ezekiel 1:14, 1★), *Voice from the Firmament* (a strike from above, 2★), *Sevenfold Verdict* (a chained strike, 3★).
   - Path at 40: **Verdict**, a stacking mark that overloads, like Deepwoken's Surge.
4. **Whirlwind** (like Galebreathe): "a whirlwind came out of the north" (Ezekiel 1:4).
   - Status: **Winded** (slower enemy attacks) and **Hushed** (enemy can't cast, like Suffocate).
   - Hymns: *Updraft* (mobility, 1★), *Noise of Great Waters* (a wing-roar that knocks back, Ezekiel 1:24, 2★), *Answer from the Whirlwind* (a cyclone, Job 40:6, 3★).
5. **Radiance** (quest-unlocked from Tier 1; the opposite of Shadowcast)
   - Status: **Dazzle** (blinds). Landing Radiance Hymns drains Hell-energy from the target and restores your Grace.
   - Hymns: *Glory Flash* (1★), *Unveiling* (reveals and strips enemy buffs, 2★), *Brightness Round About* (an aura, Ezekiel 1:27–28, 3★).
6. **Sacrifice** (quest-unlocked from Tier 1; like Bloodrend)
   - You spend your own HP for power. Status: **Offered**, which marks a target to take bonus damage.
   - Hymns: *Lamb's Blood Ward* (1★), *They Overcame* (2★), *Unto the Death* (3★; Revelation 12:11). The 2★ Hymn isn't called "Testimony", so it can't be confused with the meta currency.

**Wordless Hymns** are the equivalent of Deepwoken's attunement-less Mantras. They scale on core attributes. Example: *Trisagion* ("Holy, holy, holy," Isaiah 6:3), a Voice Hymn that shakes the ground the way "the posts of the door moved" (Isaiah 6:4).

**Canticles (the Murmurs; optional, one per character):**
- **Hosanna:** more posture damage dealt, less posture damage taken (like Ardour).
- **Selah**, a musical "pause": stealth (like Tacet).
- **Antiphon:** call-and-response that lets you track allies and enemies (like Rhythm).

## 5. Vows (the Oaths)

The rules match Deepwoken: you can hold one Vow at a time, Vow Gifts are free, Vow Hymns never level up, and there is a separate "Vow damage" type. The default is **Unsworn**, like Deepwoken's Pathfinder. Vows are broken at **the Mercy Seat**, the equivalent of Yun'Shul, and some can never be sworn again. Most Vows need Corruption Tier 2 (section 10).

1. **Vow of the Flaming Sword** (the Gate of Eden)
   - Unlock: 40 Might and the Gate quest, from Tier 2.
   - Playstyle: zone-holding with a blade. It pairs with the Flaming Sword (section 13) but works with any blade.
   - Gifts: *Ring of Flame* (your critical leaves a burning ring around you), *None Shall Pass* (a stance that punishes anyone who rolls through you), *Keeper of the Tree*.
2. **Vow of the Watcher**
   - Unlock: 40 Faith and a sleepless vigil event.
   - Playstyle: stealth and vision, the counter-Vow to the Herald (like Blindseer).
   - Gifts: *Sleepless*, *Mark of Witness* (tags a target through walls), *Veiled Eyes*.
3. **Vow of the Herald**
   - Unlock: 50 Voice.
   - Playstyle: support and control.
   - Gifts: *Annunciation* (an area buff), *Send Me* (a dash to a downed allied angel, Isaiah 6:8), *Whom Shall I Send* (summons an allied angel).
4. **Vow of the Martyr**
   - Unlock: no stat requirement; take a killing blow meant for an allied angel in a scripted quest.
   - Playstyle: gets stronger at low HP.
   - Gifts: *Loved Not Their Lives* (Revelation 12:11), *Word of Testimony*, *Last Breath Outcry*.
5. **Vow of the Ophan's Eye**
   - Unlock: 20 Wisdom, 20 Judgement-Thunder and 20 Whirlwind (a multi-stat requirement like Arcwarder).
   - Playstyle: orbiting wheel-constructs.
   - Gifts: *Wheel in the Middle of a Wheel* (Ezekiel 1:16), *Eyes Round About* (360° parry warnings), *Went Straight Forward*.
6. **Vow of the Live Coal**
   - Unlock: 15 in four different Tongues (like Saintsworn).
   - Playstyle: weaving several elements together.
   - Gifts: *Lips Touched* (cheaper Hymns), *Iniquity Taken Away*, *Altar's Tongs*.
7. **Vow of Michael's Host**
   - Unlock: 75 Medium Arms, or 90 combined across all Arms (like Bladeharper).
   - Playstyle: pure weapon mastery.
   - Gifts: *War in Heaven* (posture damage in chains), *And Prevailed Not*, *Cast Out* (the flourish throws the target).
8. **Forbidden: the Vow of the Fallen Star**
   - Unlock: Corruption Tier 5 and personal Stain of 50 or more. You take "the key of the bottomless pit" from a fallen star (Revelation 9:1).
   - Playstyle: converts Stain into power. Demons stay neutral until you strike.
   - Gifts: *Key of the Pit*, *Smoke of the Furnace* (an obscuring cloud, Revelation 9:2), *Locust Crown* (Revelation 9:7).
   - Cost: every allied angel except the altar-keepers turns hostile, and the pure endings are locked out. Breaking it at the Mercy Seat is possible once and leaves a permanent mark.

## 6. Gifts (the Talents)

**How cards are drawn (as in Deepwoken):**
- Each Ascension gives Gift hands of 4 to 5 cards, and you pick one.
- Rarities are Common, Rare (about two per Ascension) and Advanced (the ends of Talent trees).
- Some hands include a *Stay* card (Fold), which carries the hand to your next Ascension.
- **Feathers** (Aces) let you ban a card, guarantee a card, or keep a card.
- Some Gifts are mutually exclusive. Vow, Origin and Quest Gifts are free.
- Hands get harder as corruption rises: section 10 lists how each tier changes them.

**Categories:** Form, Spirit, Arms, Tongue, Vow, Testament (quest and Origin), plus two new pools:
- **Fallen:** offered only from Tier 3 and only while your Stain is 25 or more. Each Fallen Gift you take adds Stain (section 11).
- **Sanctified:** offered only while your Stain is 0. This is a counter-curve, so that staying pure also opens new options.

**Example Gifts:**
1. *Covering Wings* (Common, Steadfast 20): 10% less posture damage when blocking from the front ("with twain he covered his face," Isaiah 6:2).
2. *Burnished Feet* (Common, Steadfast 15): you can't be knocked down when you land (Ezekiel 1:7).
3. *Lion's Face* (Common, Might 25): your flourish deals +15% posture damage.
4. *Eagle's Face* (Common, Swiftness 25): +1 air dash.
5. *Full of Eyes* (Rare, Wisdom 35): enemies behind you flash when they wind up an attack.
6. *Here Am I* (Rare, Voice 30): dash to a downed allied angel and give them brief invulnerability.
7. *Live Coal Parry* (Rare, Seraph-Fire 30): a successful parry Hushes the attacker for 1.5 seconds.
8. *Turned Not* (Advanced, Swiftness 40): dashing forward grants hyperarmor, but you can't steer during it.
9. *Brimstone Lungs* (Fallen, Stain 25): your Outcry breathes fire and brimstone (Revelation 9:17–18). Each use adds 2 Stain, and Sanctity regenerates at half speed.
10. *Seal on the Forehead* (Sanctified): immune to locust-swarm damage while your Stain is 0 (Revelation 9:4).
11. *Patient Blade* (Common, Medium Arms 20): a light attack that lands within 0.5 seconds of a successful feint bait deals +10% damage.

**How corruption adds pools:**
- Tier 3 adds the Fallen Common and Rare cards.
- Tier 4 adds Fallen Advanced cards and hybrid Tongue Gifts.
- Tier 5 adds the Forbidden Vow tree.
- From Tier 2, Sanctified cards are weighted higher for players with no Stain.

## 7. Anointings (the Enchantments)

Anointings use **Sanctified Whetstones** (weapons) and **Holy Oil** (armour). The rules match Deepwoken: they drop at a small chance, an item soulbinds when equipped, you can't stack the same one twice, and they come in two kinds. Blessings appear from Tier 2. **Brands** (Curses) appear from Tier 3, and equipping a Branded item for the first time adds 10 Stain.

**Blessings:**
1. *Coal-Anointed:* M1 hits apply light Sear.
2. *Beryl Wheel:* every third parry launches a rolling wheel.
3. *Amber Glory:* landing a critical restores Grace.
4. *Watcher's Oil* (armour): better stealth, and ambushes are revealed.

**Brands:**
5. *Brand of the Locust:* +15% damage, and M1s apply **Torment** (no healing; Revelation 9:5). In return, your own healing is 25% weaker.
6. *Mark of the Number:* large posture and armour bonus, but no angelic merchant will trade with you ("no man might buy or sell, save he that had the mark," Revelation 13:16–17). Only Hell markets serve you.
7. *Leviathan Scale* (armour): M1s can't guard-break you, because the scales are sealed and "the sword… cannot hold" (Job 41:15–17, 26). In return, you can block but can never parry.
8. *Behemoth Bone* (armour): +20% armour, because "his bones are like bars of iron" (Job 40:18). In return, no air dash and shorter rolls.

## 8. Shrines of the Throne (the Deep Shrines)

Shrines are paid with **Visions**, a currency earned within a run and lost on wipe, which is the equivalent of Deepwoken's Knowledge. You earn Visions from Ascensions, first boss kills, solo boss kills and quests. They are named Visions after "I saw visions of God" (Ezekiel 1:1). Costs rise with corruption tier (section 10), and the costs below are Tier 0 costs.

1. **Shrine of the Scales** (Order)
   - Averages your stats. Costs 10 Visions and can be used once per run.
   - Needs Ascension 8 or higher and no Vow.
   - Can take at most 25 points from a core or Arms attribute, but any amount from Tongues.
2. **Shrine of Refining** (Mastery)
   - For 1 Vision, refund one attribute point (three per run at most) or move a Trait point.
   - Cannot go below the requirement of a Gift you hold.
   - New: for 3 Visions, cleanse 10 Stain, up to twice per run.
3. **Shrine of Joined Wings** (Unification): trade 2 Gifts and 5 Visions for a Hymn Draw ("their wings were joined one to another," Ezekiel 1:9).
4. **Shrine of the Parted Wings** (Division): trade a Hymn and 5 Visions for a *Two Lesser Gifts* card.
5. **Shrine of Lots** (Chance): trade a Gift and 5 Visions for a hand of the same rarity. For 10 Visions, trade a Common for a hand of Rares.
6. **The Mouth Speaking Great Things** (Blasphemy; a Hell-made shrine that only appears from Tier 3; Revelation 13:5)
   - For 5 Visions: three instant Ascensions, plus one Burden and +15 Stain.
   - Above Ascension 17, it rerolls a Burden instead.

**The Mercy Seat** (Yun'Shul) is separate from the Shrines. It breaks Vows, rerolls a Trumpet (which applies the Tarnished Burden), removes a Canticle, and can lift a Brand off an item (section 11).

## 9. Trumpets (the Resonance)

Pressing C sounds your **Trumpet** (Revelation 9:1, "the fifth angel sounded"). It works like Deepwoken's Resonance, without its PvP path:
- You build hidden **Progress** through boss kills (the best source) and by sealing Hell breaches. Deepwoken's PvP Progress is dropped, because the game is solo.
- If your HP hits zero inside a breach while your Progress is full, **the Throne answers**: a choir of trumpets sounds, you are fully healed, and you choose one of three cards. When the Throne answers, you aren't struck down, so your halo doesn't dim and you don't lose a life.
- Trumpet points are randomly spread across each Trumpet's sub-stats.
- Trumpets unlock at Tier 4 (section 10).

**Rarities:** Normal; **Cracked** (Corrupted); **Archangel** (Legendary); **Abyssal** (a Cracked Archangel).

**Examples:**
1. *Thy Sin Purged* (Normal): heals allies in an area and purges their ailments ("thine iniquity is taken away, and thy sin purged," Isaiah 6:6–7).
2. *Wheel within a Wheel* (Normal): an eye-wheel orbits you and deflects projectiles (Ezekiel 1:16–18).
3. *Terrible Crystal* (Normal): a brief crystal dome that blocks attacks (Ezekiel 1:22).
4. *Voice of the Almighty* (Cracked): a huge wing-roar that stuns, but each use costs 40 Sanctity, like Deepwoken's Mania downside ("as the noise of great waters, as the voice of the Almighty," Ezekiel 1:24).
5. *Michael's Charge* (Archangel): teleport and strike a marked foe (Revelation 12:7).
6. *The Pit Opened* (Abyssal): smoke that "darkened the sun" and a locust swarm. Each use costs 10% of your max HP as Ash, a Wither-like effect, and adds 5 Stain (Revelation 9:2–3).

## 10. Corruption tiers: the power curve

This system is new and has no Deepwoken equivalent. **World Corruption** is the run's clock.

**How it rises (decided).** Heaven decays at its own rate, driven by time. World Corruption rises at a steady, designed rate, and nothing the player does lowers it. As starting values: an in-game day lasts about 30 minutes of play, and a new tier begins every four in-game days, so about every 2 hours, with Tier 5 arriving about 10 hours into a run. That pace depends on the plan's open question about how long a run lasts. *This reading of Dan's "decays at its own rate" is an interpretation that Dan can adjust.*

**What breaches do.** Breaches don't change the world's tier, but they change their own region:
- Each region has a local corruption level that follows the world tier.
- An open breach pulls its region up to one tier ahead of the world: harder enemies, but also that tier's loot.
- Sealing a breach slows the local spread and protects the region. A sealed region stays one tier behind the world, with a minimum of Tier 0. Its altar and merchants stay safe, and its quests, Shrine of the Throne and region rewards stay open.
- Sealing a breach also fills Trumpet Progress, pays Visions, and cleanses 3 Stain.

So the choice in each region is between safety (seal it and fall behind on loot) and greed (leave it open for better drops and harder fights).

**No strength cap (decided).** Your Ascension isn't capped by tier. The challenge is to keep pace with Heaven's decay by growing stronger. Each tier lists an **Ascension pace**: where a player should be to keep up. These are targets for balancing, not limits. A strong player can get ahead of the curve, and a slow one falls behind and feels it.

**Separately**, the player carries their own **Stain**, from 0 to 100 (section 11).

## 11. Stain and the Fall

**Stain** is personal corruption, from 0 to 100. You take it on for power, and it is separate from lives: being struck down never adds Stain, and Stain never costs a life. The Watcher-kin flaw multiplies every source by 1.25.

### Sources of Stain

All amounts are starting values.

| Source | Stain |
| --- | --- |
| Eating a demon heart (heals 30% of your HP) | +4 each |
| Equipping a Branded item for the first time | +10 per item |
| Taking a Fallen Gift | +6 Common, +10 Rare, +15 Advanced |
| The Mouth Speaking Great Things | +15 per use |
| *Brimstone Lungs* Outcry | +2 per use |
| *The Pit Opened* (Abyssal Trumpet) | +5 per use |
| Buying from a Hell market | +2 per purchase |
| Fallen Watcher knockdown (at 50 Stain or more) | +2 each |
| Seventh Watcher (rare variant) | Starts at 10 |

### Cleansing Stain

| Way to cleanse | Stain removed | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shrine of Refining | −10 | 3 Visions, at most twice per run |
| Sealing a breach | −3 | None, but only once per breach |
| A seraph's coal at an altar ("thy sin purged," Isaiah 6:7) | −15 | 4 Visions and 25% of your max HP as a burn. Once per tier |
| Slaying a marked corrupted angel (the Penitent's bounties) | −5 | The fight |
| Lifting a Brand at the Mercy Seat | Half of that Brand's Stain | The item loses its Brand |

### Thresholds

The effects at 25 and 50 lift if you cleanse back below them, with a small margin so they don't flicker: 25 lifts below 20, and 50 lifts below 40. The effects at 75 stay for the rest of the run.

- **0, pure:** Sanctified Gifts are offered, and *Seal on the Forehead* works.
- **25, a cracked halo:**
  - From Tier 3, Fallen Gifts appear in Gift hands.
  - Your Sanctity cap drops by 10%.
  - Hell enemies notice you from 10% closer, because you smell a little like them.
- **50, your Choir falls:**
  - Your Choir becomes its fallen variant (section 2): the same stats, with the passive turned toward Hell.
  - Allied angels charge 30% more, and one in four refuses to serve you.
  - Altar-keepers still serve you and still clear a dimmed halo, so your lives work exactly as before.
- **75, Hell's regard:**
  - Hell's elites hesitate for 0.5 seconds before their first attack on you.
  - Fallen Gifts are 10% stronger.
  - Sanctity regenerates 25% more slowly.
  - Half of the allied angels turn hostile, but never the altar-keepers.
  - The pure endings are locked for the rest of the run.
- **100, the Fall:** the run ends.

### The Fall (decided)

Reaching 100 Stain ends the run. You fall out of Heaven, become a fallen angel, and start over. It's a wipe, like being cast out: the character, the build, the Visions and every change to the map are gone. Testimony and Remembrance still carry over, and the Fall itself is a Witness.

> The last crack runs all the way around the ring, and the halo goes red from the inside, like iron in a forge. For a moment nothing happens. Then the ground of Heaven stops holding you. You drop through the white stone as if it were smoke, past the gates, past the Sea of Glass, past the first breach you ever sealed. Your wings catch fire, and then they catch the dark. Far below, Hell's red light comes up to meet you, and something there knows your name. The screen goes black, and a single line of text appears: *"How art thou fallen from heaven"* (Isaiah 14:12). Then the kindling begins again, and a new Tenth opens their eyes in the Outer Courts.

**How Stain and lives fit together.** Lives and Stain are two separate tracks, and the Fall doesn't change how lives work:
- Being struck down the first time dims your halo, and the second time casts you out. Stain doesn't change this.
- Reaching 100 Stain ends the run even if you still have both lives.
- Being struck down never adds Stain, and cleansing Stain never restores a life.
- If an Abyssal Trumpet would push you to 100 while the Throne is answering, the Fall happens.

### The halo in the UI

The halo shows both tracks at once, on separate channels, so a player can read either one at a glance:
- **Lives change the halo's brightness.** A full halo glows at full brightness, and a dimmed halo glows at about half. The dimmed part of the health bar shows the same thing.
- **Stain changes its cracks and colour.** Hairline cracks appear from 1 Stain and grow with it. Red seeps in from the cracks: a visible crack at 25, half the ring red with embers at 50, and almost all red, with a flicker, at 75. At 100 the ring shatters, and the Fall scene plays.
- **A number on the character screen** shows exact Stain, with the next threshold marked.
- Red is Hell's colour in the plan's palette, so a reddening halo reads as danger without explanation.

### Optional, for later: your fallen self as a nemesis

*Not decided, and not planned for any milestone yet.* After a run ends in the Fall, your fallen self could appear in your next run as a named corrupted-angel elite, with your old Choir's fallen variant, your weapon and some of your Gifts. Defeating it would grant a large Testimony bonus. It's kept here as an idea for Dan to pick up or drop later.

## 12. Testimony and Remembrance: what's kept on a wipe (the Echoes)

**Testimony** is the meta currency, named after "the word of their testimony" (Revelation 12:11). It comes from one-time **Witnesses** (Triumphs) completed in a run. Examples:
- Swore a Vow: 5
- Heard a Trumpet: 15
- Defeated Abaddon: 5
- Fell at 100 Stain: 5
- Each Ascension: 0.75

Ranks run from E to S as in Deepwoken. **Penances** (Modifiers) multiply the Testimony you earn. Examples: *Fragile Heart* (double damage taken), *Clipped Choir* (no Trumpet use), *Fated* (cards are picked for you).

**Remembrance** is the knowledge that persists. It is earned by doing things, not bought:
- **Bestiary:** each enemy has three tiers.
  - *Seen* gives the name and silhouette.
  - *Studied* lists its attacks, including which are unparryable or are grabs (earned by parrying or dodging each move a few times). It also shows the enemy's read trait and which responses it uses, once you've baited it with a feint or been punished by it for one (section 14).
  - *Mastered* shows its Tongue weakness and posture threshold (earned by killing it at least 5 times), and its read delay and, for adaptive enemies, how many wind-ups it remembers.
- **Map memory:** landmarks you've discovered stay as faded ink on the next run's map.
- **Lore:** pages and scripture fragments in a codex. Some reveal quest routes, such as where to find the Gate quest.

**Unlocks:**
- Radiance and Sacrifice stay learned for the account.
- Origins.
- The mace as a fourth starter weapon (section 13).
- Rare Choir variants, each for 40 Testimony once its Remembrance condition is met (section 2). They are never rolled.
- **Vow skip:** a Vow you've fully progressed before can be skipped later for 10 Visions.
- The **Reliquary** lets you pass down one Anointed item for a high Testimony cost.

**Small capped upgrades** (Deepwoken-scale):
- +2% damage against demons per step, up to 8%.
- +2% armour, up to 8%.
- +1 starting Vision per step, up to 5.
- +1 starting Feather, up to 2.

Because knowledge and options do most of the work, the stat creep stays small.

## 13. Weapons

All weapons share the same controls: left-click for the M1 chain (hits followed by a flourish), F to parry, hold F to block, right-click to feint during a wind-up, and R for the critical, which can't be feinted. Timing numbers are starting values, and the sword is the baseline the others are tuned against.

### Starter weapons

Starter weapons are basic and Plain tier. The sword, spear and knife are available from the first run, and the mace is unlocked with Testimony.

1. **Sword** (Medium). The baseline, and the first weapon built.
   - *M1 chain:* three even hits and a flourish, with a wind-up of about 0.35 seconds.
   - *Parry feel:* the standard 150-millisecond window, with a forgiving recovery.
   - *R critical, Rising Cut:* an upward cut that deals 1.5 times a light hit's posture damage. 6-second cooldown.
2. **Spear** (Medium).
   - *M1 chain:* three thrusts and a sweeping flourish, with about 40% more reach than the sword and a slower wind-up of about 0.42 seconds.
   - *Parry feel:* a 140-millisecond window. You parry at the shaft's length, so you can parry from slightly farther away.
   - *R critical, Brace:* a planted thrust that stops a charging enemy and knocks it back. 7-second cooldown.
3. **Knife** (Light).
   - *M1 chain:* four quick hits and a flourish, with a wind-up of about 0.25 seconds. Low posture damage.
   - *Parry feel:* a short 120-millisecond window, but a parry cooldown about 25% shorter than the sword's.
   - *R critical, Slip:* a short step forward and a stab that hits twice. 5-second cooldown.
4. **Mace** (Heavy). Unlocked with Testimony.
   - *M1 chain:* three slow, heavy hits and a flourish, with a wind-up of about 0.55 seconds. High posture damage.
   - *Parry feel:* a 160-millisecond window, but a longer recovery if you miss.
   - *R critical, Overhead:* a slow overhead smash that deals heavy posture damage to a blocking target. 8-second cooldown.

A short bow was considered as a starter and left out: the Bow of Light covers ranged play later, and a ranged starter would weaken the parry-first learning curve.

### Progression weapons

These are never starting weapons. You find or earn them as you grow stronger, and each is gated by a corruption tier and a quest or source. They drop at Hallowed tier or better (section 10).

| Weapon | Class | First tier | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spear of the Host | Medium | 1 | Rising through the Host of the Gate's ranks |
| Flaming Sword | Medium | 2 | The Gate of Eden quest |
| Censer Flail | Heavy | 2 | A Throne-region quest |
| Ophan Cestus | Light gauntlets | 2 | Dropped by the corrupted ophan elite |
| Bow of Light | Light, ranged | 3 | The Radiance quest line |
| Herald's Horn | Heavy, catalyst | 3 | The Vow of the Herald |
| Cedar Tail | Heavy | 4 | Dropped by Behemoth |
| Blade of Michael's Host | Medium | 5 | Carried by the dragon's lieutenants |

1. **Spear of the Host** (Medium)
   - Long reach and slightly slower M1s.
   - Critical: a lunge that leaves the spear planted. You can follow up by pulling it out.
2. **Flaming Sword** (Medium)
   - An even 3+1 chain and a forgiving parry window, like the sword, with white and gold fire on every hit.
   - Critical: *Turning Every Way*, a wide sweep that deals posture damage.
   - It ties into the Vow of the Flaming Sword.
3. **Censer Flail** (Heavy)
   - Slow M1s with very high posture damage.
   - Critical: a swing that releases a smoke cloud ("the house was filled with smoke," Isaiah 6:4), which blocks enemy sight. Best for guard breaks.
4. **Ophan Cestus** (Light gauntlets)
   - Fast four-hit chain and a short-range parry.
   - Critical: spinning rings that hit several times.
5. **Bow of Light** (Light, ranged)
   - The M1 chain is quick draws, and the flourish is a point-blank knockback.
   - Parrying is weaker, so it leans on rolling.
   - Critical: a charged piercing shot.
6. **Herald's Horn** (Heavy, catalyst)
   - Bell-mouth bashes, and blocking with it builds Fervour.
   - Critical: a sonic blast that deals posture damage and briefly applies Hushed.
7. **Cedar Tail** (Heavy)
   - A flail-maul with a long, sweeping chain that hits everything in front of you.
   - Critical: a stiff-tailed slam that knocks down anything without hyperarmor.
8. **Blade of Michael's Host** (Medium)
   - The strongest blade in the game, with a fast 3+1 chain and very high posture damage.
   - Critical: a rising strike that throws the target, like the *Cast Out* Gift.

## 14. Combat controls and feints

**Controls (decided).** WASD moves, the mouse aims, and left-click is the M1 chain. F parries, holding F blocks, Q rolls and R is the critical. **Right-click is always the feint**, and it also cancels a Hymn cast. There are no right-click or middle-click criticals: R is the only critical input. Criticals can't be feinted.

In Deepwoken, feints matter mostly against other players. Here they must work against the AI, so every enemy reads your wind-ups, commits to a response, and can be baited. All numbers in this section are starting values to tune.

### The feint

- **Window:** right-click on any frame of an M1 wind-up before its active frames, or during a Hymn cast before it's released.
- **Recovery:** a feint returns you to neutral after 0.1 seconds, so it isn't free.
- **Cooldown:** after a feint, you can't feint again for 1 second.
- **Limit:** you can feint at most twice in one M1 chain. The third wind-up in the same chain can't be feinted.
- **Reward:** a feint that baits a committed enemy gives +10 Fervour. A feint that baits nothing gives nothing.

### How enemies read you

When you start a wind-up within an enemy's threat range, the enemy **reads** it:

1. **Read delay.** After a short delay, the enemy decides whether to respond.
2. **Commit chance.** The enemy rolls against its commit chance. If it commits, it picks a response from its own response table: parry, block, roll or counter. Each enemy type has its own weights, kept in the bestiary data.
3. **Commitment.** Once committed, the enemy can't change its mind until the response plays out. Each commitment shows a clear tell (a red parry glint, a raised guard, a crouch before a roll, or an orange counter telegraph), so the player can see when a bait worked.
4. **If you don't commit and it waits,** it may punish your feint instead (see the read traits).

### What a bait wins

| The enemy committed to | What happens when you feint | Your opening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parry | Its parry whiffs, goes on a 1.5-second cooldown, and leaves it in 0.35 seconds of recovery | Your next M1 in that window can't be parried and deals +25% posture damage |
| Block | It holds its guard for at least 0.6 seconds | Hits on a raised guard fill its posture, and your R critical deals 1.5 times its posture damage to a held block |
| Roll | It rolls into empty space, and its i-frames run out before your real attack. Its roll goes on a 2.5-second cooldown | Your first follow-up M1 tracks the roll, with up to 30° of aim help and a 1-metre lunge |
| Counter | It starts a counter-attack with an orange telegraph | You're free to parry it, and a parried counter stuns it 0.5 seconds longer than a normal parry |

### Read traits

Every enemy has one read trait. Each trait's numbers can shift by enemy type, and they tighten a little as corruption rises.

| Trait | Read delay | Commit chance | How it handles feints | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gullible | 0.10 s | 80% | Almost always commits, always falls for a feint, and never learns | Imps, locust scouts, Goetic Knights |
| Wary | 0.25 s | 35% | Waits until late in your wind-up, so only a late feint baits it. If it sees a feint without committing, it attacks into your recovery 50% of the time | Presidents, Counts, crowned locusts, Leviathan |
| Adaptive | 0.15 s | 60%, falling as it learns | Learns your feint habits (see below) | Legion, corrupted angels, Goetic Kings, Abaddon, the dragon, the beasts |
| Unreading | – | 0% | Never reacts to wind-ups, so feints do nothing. Break it with posture or Hymns instead | Locust swarms, Behemoth |

**How adaptive enemies learn.**
- An adaptive enemy remembers your last 6 wind-ups, or 8 from Tier 4.
- If more than half of them were feints, its commit chance drops to 20%, and it attacks into your feint recovery 70% of the time.
- It also learns your **rhythm**. If three of your feints cancel within 60 milliseconds of the same point in the wind-up, it learns that point and won't commit before it.
- The memory fades. A real hit that lands counts as a non-feint, and each 4 seconds without a wind-up forgets one entry. Mixing real attacks with feints, and changing their timing, keeps an adaptive enemy guessing.
- Legion's bodies share one memory, so feint habits that fool one body stop working on all of them.

### Feints and Remembrance

The bestiary shows how each enemy reads (section 12):
- *Seen:* nothing about reads yet.
- *Studied:* the read trait's icon and the responses it uses. It's earned by baiting the enemy once or being punished by it for a feint.
- *Mastered:* its read delay, shown as a tick on your own wind-up bar while you target it, and, for an adaptive enemy, how many wind-ups it remembers.

The Wheelborn's Rings of Eyes builds this knowledge 25% faster, and the *Patient Blade* Gift rewards landing a hit right after a bait.
